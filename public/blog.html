Buying property in Zimbabwe

Begin with a clear budget and a realistic view of the costs involved. Shortlist locations and property types that suit your needs, then compare available options rather than rushing into the first opportunity.

Before paying a deposit, establish who owns the property and who is authorised to sell it. Ask for the relevant title, deed, allocation or other supporting documents and have them independently reviewed by a qualified Zimbabwean legal professional. Confirm the agreed payment, transfer and handover process in writing.

What to check before buying a stand

Ask what kind of land is being offered, who is authorised to sell it, what ownership or allocation documents exist, and whether subdivision and development approvals apply. Confirm access to roads, water, electricity and other services rather than relying only on verbal assurances.

Urban, peri-urban and rural land can have different planning and legal requirements. Have the relevant documents checked by qualified professionals before making payment.

Finding a rental property

Compare the full cost of renting, including deposit, rent, utilities, maintenance responsibilities and any other agreed charges. Inspect the property and record its condition before moving in.

Read the lease carefully. Make sure the rent, payment dates, duration, notice period, deposit arrangements and responsibilities of both parties are clear and documented.

Preparing your property for the market

Prepare accurate details about the property, location, asking price, size, features and availability. Gather the documents that help establish your authority to sell or let the property.

Good photographs, clear access for viewings and prompt responses to enquiries can help prospective clients assess the opportunity. Agree marketing arrangements and fees in writing before work begins.

Assessing a property investment

Consider the purchase price alongside realistic rental demand, vacancy risk, repairs, rates, taxes, management costs and future capital requirements. Verify any claims about income or occupancy with supporting records.

Property investments carry risks. Seek independent legal, valuation and financial advice appropriate to the transaction.

Understanding commercial property

Review the permitted use, access, parking, utilities, building condition, compliance requirements and suitability for the intended business activity. If the property is tenanted, examine the lease terms and payment history.

Independent inspections and professional advice can help identify obligations or costs that may not be obvious during an initial viewing.
